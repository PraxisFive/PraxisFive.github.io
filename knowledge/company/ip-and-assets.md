# IP and Reusable Assets

PraxisFive's models, frameworks, and tools are **the firm's core asset**. How they're protected in contracting, and how they compound into margin.

> Established in the [2026-08-05 operating model working session](../meetings/2026-08-05-operating-model-working-session.md) — the most consistently emphasized thread of that session.

---

## The position

**Our models are ours.** A client engaging PraxisFive gets the benefit of the AI Integrated Operating Model, the assessment frameworks, and the tooling — they can leverage it, adapt it, and make it their own inside their business.

**They do not acquire it.**

The operative test:

> If we build something for one client, we must remain free to do the same work for another — including a direct competitor — unless that client has explicitly and by name restricted it.

A client may negotiate a named exclusion. That is a commercial term with a price attached, not a default.

---

## Why this needs handling at contract time

**Derivative rights are the trap.** Standard consulting agreements frequently grant the client ownership of derivative works. Applied to a framework-based firm, that clause quietly transfers the asset — because everything we deliver is a derivative of a model we brought with us.

The distinction that has to survive into every contract:

| What the client gets | What PraxisFive retains |
|---------------------|--------------------------|
| The specific deliverable, built for their context | The underlying model, framework, or tool |
| Full rights to use, adapt, and operationalize it internally | The right to reuse the underlying IP with other clients |
| Their own data, findings, and results | The generalized method and any improvements to it |

**Set this at contract, not at renewal.** Retrofitting it is a renegotiation; establishing it up front is a clause.

---

## The asset library

The frameworks exist and are accumulating. The discipline is keeping them **generalized** rather than letting each engagement fork them into something bespoke.

Assets in play or forming:

- **The AI Integrated Operating Model** — the five mega flows. See [AI Integrated Operating Model](ai-operating-model.md).
- **The assessment framework** — four levels of assessment object: **Partner · Portfolio · Program · People.** Scales from a single entity to thousands, and ports across object types (partners, clients, reps, sales teams). Quantitative (e-surveys) plus qualitative (interview guides).
- **The Value Assessment → Roadmap → Execution Tracks chain** — the integrated tool spine. See [Engagement architecture](engagement-architecture.md).
- **Templates** — every reusable artifact, deliberately built for repeatability.

---

## Build non-bespoke, on purpose

Real client work is the source of the asset library — but only if it's built to generalize.

**Build client work non-bespoke.** Use cases developed in engagements are built deliberately non-bespoke, so they become reusable assets rather than one client's custom artifacts.

The test to apply while building, not after:

> *Would this work for a different client in a different industry with the client-specific layer swapped out?*

If no, it's a delivery artifact. If yes, it's an asset — and it goes in the library.

---

## The economic link

This is not a legal preference. It's the margin mechanism.

The project margin target in [Economic model](economic-model.md) (70% gross at its latest setting, and still contested) is unreachable if every engagement rebuilds from zero. Reuse is what converts a services business into one with leverage — and the reason the asset library gets treated as a balance-sheet item rather than a byproduct.

> *"For us to make money, we've got to be able to reuse stuff."*
