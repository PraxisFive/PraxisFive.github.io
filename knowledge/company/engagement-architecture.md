# Engagement Architecture

How a PraxisFive engagement is structured, regardless of size. Three parts: **where the client is** (journey stage), **what we build** (the spine), and **how the work is sequenced** (execution tracks).

> The number five recurs deliberately: five mega flows, five journey stages, five execution tracks, five offering sizes.

---

## Where the client is — the five journey stages

Every engagement is positioned against where the client actually sits on their AI journey. The stage sets the ambition, the pace, and which offering size fits.

> **Replaced 2026-08-07.** The brand document's five-level journey supersedes the earlier Initiate → Accelerate → Optimize → Scale → Transform set. See [Brand](brand.md).

| Level | The client's situation |
|-------|------------------------|
| **1 — Emerging** | Experiments and pilots. AI is isolated, value is anecdotal. |
| **2 — Developing** | Repeatable use cases. Foundations forming, value is local. |
| **3 — Proficient** | AI embedded in core workflows. Value is measured and real. |
| **4 — Differentiated** | AI reshapes how the business competes. Value compounds. |
| **5 — Exponential** | AI redefines the operating model. Value scales non-linearly. |

**Each level isn't a step — it's a magnitude.** Levels 4 and 5 are where value scales non-linearly, which is why the gap between them and level 3 is the one worth pricing against.

Level is assessed, not asserted. A client's self-identified level and their evidenced level are often different — that gap is frequently the most useful finding in the baseline. **Most organizations sit at different levels in different domains**, so the read is per-flow, not a single score.

### How we engage against it

- **ASSESS** — where are you today? Maturity diagnosed across all five domains, not just technology.
- **PARTNER** — no handoff at deployment. We stay through adoption, measurement, and the next level up.
- **ELEVATE** — one level at a time. Level 5 isn't the entry point; durable gains come from advancing deliberately, not skipping stages.

## What we build — the spine

Every engagement, at every size, is built from the same three components. What changes across sizes is depth, breadth, and duration — not shape.

### 1. Baseline / Assessment / Benchmarks
Where the client is today. Assessed across all five mega flows, benchmarked, and evidenced. This establishes the starting point, identifies the dominant mega flow for the engagement, and sizes the opportunity.

### 2. Roadmap / Blueprints / Action Plans
The path from current state to desired state. What to do, in what order, with what sequencing logic and what expected value at each step.

### 3. Execution Tracks
The actual work, sorted into five tracks by size of prize and time horizon. See below.

**The spine is what we sell and what we deliver.** Baseline + Roadmap + Execution Tracks, packaged in five sizes. See [Service offerings](service-offerings.md).

### The spine is packaged as three accelerators

**Established 2026-08-05 (session 2).** An **accelerator** is a packaged module that plugs into the base service offering at each t-shirt size. The three accelerators map one-to-one onto the spine:

| # | Accelerator | Varies by size? |
|---|-------------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Contextual Analysis** — the baseline | No |
| 2 | **Roadmap / Blueprint** | No |
| 3 | **Execution Tracks** | **Yes — this is the only variable** |

> **Names settled 2026-08-10** (Lorin, editing the master deck): accelerator 1 is **Contextual Analysis** — "contextual" doing real work, since contextual relevance is one of the four ethos commitments. Accelerator 3 is **Execution Tracks**, matching the five tracks it contains.

On the naming of the first: *"I don't want to get too hung up if it's a baseline, a diagnostic, an assessment, a benchmark."* The job is the same — discover where they are and evidence it, in context.

**Three and only three, on purpose.** From Lorin's AppDynamics experience: a large catalogue of packaged services meant salespeople couldn't explain the offer, and buyers treated the spec sheets as a menu — cherry-picking parts of each. *"It got really convoluted. Keep it simple, because that's exactly where it makes it easier when you're trying to sell these."*

**Still to build (Marc):** the definition of each accelerator, plus level of effort **per customer segment × per t-shirt size**. Level of effort has to differ by segment because cost to serve differs by segment — see [Ideal client profile](ideal-client-profile.md).

### The spine is also a tool chain

The three components aren't three deliverables produced in sequence by hand — they are **one integrated chain**, where each stage feeds the next automatically:

**Value Assessment → Roadmap → Execution Tracks**

The assessment output populates the roadmap; the roadmap resolves into sequenced execution tracks. Building this as connected tooling rather than as three documents is what makes the engagement fast enough to price the way we price it. See [IP and assets](ip-and-assets.md).

**How the baseline gets built.** Quantitative and qualitative together — e-surveys for breadth, interview guides for depth, and direct data access wherever the client will grant it. The method scales from one entity to thousands and ports across assessment objects (**Partner · Portfolio · Program · People**).

Clients still expect interviews, and interviews still surface things data doesn't. What changes is that we arrive with a **hypothesis and a point of view already formed** from their data — so the engagement starts at validation rather than at discovery.

## The front end: how Contextual Analysis actually runs

> From Lorin's walk-through of *"my instincts when I first walk in the door"* ([2026-09-09](../meetings/2026-09-09-website-and-engagement-framework.md)), plus the workflow-documentation thread ([2026-08-11](../meetings/2026-08-11-design-and-brand-working-session.md)). Marc asked for it in writing so the tooling can be built around it.

### 1. Homework, before any conversation

*"You can't say, I haven't met with them, so I don't know."* Learn as much as public information allows before the first meeting.

- **Firmographics first.** Size, how they make money, whether they are low-margin/high-volume or high-margin. *"Every business is a little different."*
- **The 5Cs:** **Company · Customer · Collaborators · Competitors · Climate** (the market). Includes reading competitors' sites and looking for the tailwinds and headwinds in the market.
- **Together, the 5Cs give the "Big C": Context.** That is literally what the first accelerator's name means. *Contextual* Analysis is the 5Cs worked through for this client.
- **Leave the homework with a hypothesis and a point of view.** Hold it honestly: *"I try really hard to say don't be biased. Even though I'm biased."*

### 2. Waterfall questioning, strategic first

Questions start at the top and work down. You only chase an anomaly once you know it matters to them.

1. **Strategic goals.** The revenue goal, then revenue by **segment, product and geography**.
2. **Economics.** Profit and margin.
3. **Resources.** Sales-team composition, and the current mix across channels and routes.
4. **Then the anomalies.** *"If the goal is the channel is 60% of revenue and it's 20% … why is the channel not performing?"* Why does one channel partner or seller succeed while the others don't?

**Question sets differ by persona.** There is a generic bank plus one set per role (CRO, CMO, CFO, and so on). The target output of a first meeting is **something specific enough to get their attention or earn a proposal**: a bottleneck, a missed revenue pocket, a stalled route.

> **Tooling status (2026-09-09):** Jesse has started a **lightweight assessment tool**. It's interactive, works on mobile, uses branching logic, and puts a dollar figure on each answer (*"how much is it costing you?"*). It's waiting on **Lorin's question bank**. Lorin's step one sits a level above Jesse's draft. Brian is strong on functional interview guides and a named contributor.

### 3. Document the real workflow: step zero

The thread that ran through August: **no process, no agent.** An agent built against an undocumented process misses the judgment calls and the edge cases, like the rule "Janet" carries in her head.

- **Reconstruct the process from completed work, not from what people say they do.** *"How it should be done and how it's actually done are two different things."* (Jesse)
- **Expect documentation to take longer than the build.** A practitioner article the team adopted claims a **3:1** ratio of documenting to building. That claim is unsourced; see [assumptions](../decisions/assumptions.md).
- **Verbatims are evidence.** *"Executives love verbatims."* Interview transcripts are data. Qualitative findings have to stay traceable into the blueprint and into governance, not just the numbers.
- **The floor value of any engagement:** *"The worst you get at the end of it is we documented it for you."*

> **Still open:** a single, named PraxisFive method for decomposing any workflow. Lorin asked whether *"there's a five-step process we use"*. Jesse's current practice is: the five repeated tasks → map every step → list the decision points under each. Lorin calls it business process reengineering renewed. See [open-questions](../decisions/open-questions.md).

### 4. What the blueprint must look like

Lorin, [2026-08-11](../meetings/2026-08-11-design-and-brand-working-session.md) and [2026-08-13](../meetings/2026-08-13-offering-packaging-with-marc.md):

- **A documented plan, not a slide.** *"You're going from here to here, and this month these are the five things you need to do, and these are the actions, and here's the owners."* Gantt-style, month by month.
- **A priority map inside it.** What can deliver results in *hours, days, weeks or months*. These horizons feed the execution tracks below.
- **Automated as far as possible.** The assessment output populates it; see the tool chain above.

### 5. The promise and the shape

- **Value visible within 30 days.** Within about six weeks, *"something they haven't seen before."* The first engagement is deliberately focused: a managed-risk bite, and more only if the client wants it.
- **An end-to-end framework for the ~6-week engagement is in draft (Marc).** Every step has a gate, and every gate has a decision. Specific offerings get retrofitted into it once it's agreed.

## How the work is sequenced — the five execution tracks

Every roadmap sorts its initiatives into these five tracks. The mix is what makes an engagement contextually relevant — a Level-1 Emerging client is heavy on Quick Strikes and Quick Wins; a Level-5 Exponential client is weighted toward Strategic Plays and Transformational Bets.

**The tracks are time-bound.** Horizons confirmed by Lorin in the [2026-08-05 operating model working session](../meetings/2026-08-05-operating-model-working-session.md).

| Track | Duration | Always delivered? | What it is |
|-------|----------|-------------------|------------|
| **Quick Strikes** | A sprint | ✅ | Immediate, near-zero-risk actions. Value visible almost at once. |
| **Quick Wins** | A sprint | ✅ | Contained, high-confidence improvements with minimal dependency on other work. |
| **Critical Priorities** | One sprint | ✅ | The things that must be fixed. Not optional, not deferrable — usually blocking value elsewhere. |
| **Strategic Plays** | **~3 months** | Optional | Deliberate moves that build durable advantage. Meaningful investment, meaningful return. |
| **Transformational Bets** | **6 months+** | Optional | Operating-model-level change. Highest risk, highest ceiling, requires executive conviction. |

**A sprint is up to four weeks**, milestone-gated. *"You can decide to not go forward, but if you prove it, you go to the next one."*

**The first three are the standing offer** — every client gets Quick Strikes, Quick Wins, and Critical Priorities. Strategic Plays and Transformational Bets are the special cases layered on top.

> **Revised 2026-08-05 (session 2).** These durations replace the earlier hours/days → month-plus set, and resolve the flag that "a month and beyond" was too short for operating-model-level change. The first three tracks remain genuinely compressed; the last two are now sized honestly.

The first two tracks are how we honor the **fast value** commitment in the ethos. The last two are how we get to **5x minimum ROI** and beyond.

**A typical S engagement is weighted to Quick Strikes and Quick Wins.** Critical Priorities get *outlined and sequenced* at that size rather than executed — the sprint itself is scoped, not run, unless the client buys deeper.

## The through-line

Every engagement resolves to the same question: **did the client realize the value?** Not was the assessment thorough, not was the roadmap sound, not were the deliverables shipped. Value realization is the North Star, and the Continuous AI Realization Service exists precisely because realization doesn't end when the engagement does.
