# Ideal Client Profile

PraxisFive works with **mid-market companies facing the AI transition**, reached through two avenues and concentrated in six focus industries.

The core insight that carries forward: **a sponsor is the influencer to the company.** A PE firm is to its portco what a family office is to a family business. Where a sponsor exists, the sponsor-backed operating upgrade is the cleanest way in. But a sponsor is now a strong signal, not a hard filter — any mid-market company has this need.

## The two avenues

### Avenue 1 — Sponsor-backed: PE and growth equity *(primary, established)*

Private equity-backed, PE-track, or growth-equity-backed companies. Also pre-IPO and recently-acquired portcos. This is the most developed avenue and the one with the deepest existing relationships.

**What changed from prior positioning:** the scope is no longer post-contract only. We now engage on the full lifecycle — new revenue and existing revenue, new customers and existing customers — across all five mega flows.

### Avenue 2 — Sponsor-backed: family offices & family-owned businesses *(forming)*

Family-owned businesses and the family offices that back them (family office ≈ $500M–$1B+ in assets), plus independently-wealthy principals connected to those networks.

**The wedge — legacy protection.** Family owners do not want to be the generation that loses control of the business. A tech-savvy next generation is pushing hard on AI and "why are we still doing it this way," while the older generation guards continuity. PraxisFive comes in as the trusted operator who helps them modernize *without* risking the legacy — precious with their money, not cavalier with it.

The broadened industry focus helps here materially: family-owned businesses are frequently in Services, FMCG, Packaging, and Materials rather than software. The prior software-only thesis didn't transfer cleanly to this avenue. This one does.

**Warm intros through family-office networks tend to be more trust-rich and more personal than PE intros.**

### The cost-to-serve trade-off — unresolved

Marc's argument against going straight to the top: **cost to serve inverts by tier.**

- **Enterprise** — you tell the sales story *once*, then implement it *ten times*. Specialists are deep, capable, and numerous. Seven specialists to coordinate instead of one generalist.
- **Growth / portfolio** — one person holds seven jobs. Far more handholding, so cost to serve rises. *"Their amount of handholding is going to be a lot higher. So our costs will go up."*

Lorin's counter: dealing with one person doing seven jobs is simpler than coordinating seven. **Neither position won.** Level of effort per accelerator now has to be modelled *per segment*, which is exactly what Marc took away as an action.

## The core signal

**They have a working business. What they don't have is an operating model built for the AI era.**

They know AI matters. They may have pockets of it working. What they lack is the rewiring — the integrated operating model that lets them actually capture the opportunity rather than bolt tools onto an unchanged business.

## Signals by mega flow

Fit shows up differently depending on where the value is. Any one of these is a legitimate entry point:

**1 — Planning.** Strategy doesn't survive contact with the operating plan. Annual planning consumes a quarter and produces misalignment. Functional plans and budgets don't ladder to corporate strategy. Joint business plans with partners exist on paper only.

**2 — Innovation & portfolio.** New offerings take too long from ideation to market. No clear view of the portfolio across incubation, transformation, performance, and productivity. Cross-functional stakeholders aren't in the room together. Alpha/beta motion is ad hoc.

**3 — Ecosystem.** No coherent read on the business ecosystem they operate in. Weak influence in their customers' ecosystem. Partner relationships that generate activity but not value creation, delivery, or capture.

**4 — Go-to-market.** Funnel velocity is poor at top, middle, or bottom. Friction between stages. Post-close adoption and expansion are a scramble. Speed without direction — motion that isn't converting to realized value.

**5 — Infrastructure & readiness.** Systems, data, or processes can't support AI at scale. Workforce capability gaps. Still shaped like a pyramid when the work calls for a diamond. AI mandate landed with no foundation under it.

## Trigger events — when they call

- Board or sponsor mandate on AI with no credible plan behind it
- New PE sponsor post-acquisition building a value creation plan
- Annual planning cycle approaching and last year's process was painful
- New executive in seat who needs a defensible baseline fast
- Preparing for a raise, exit, or refinancing and the operating story has to hold up
- Growth has stalled and there's no clean read on why
- A competitor moved first on AI and it's visible in the market
- Next-generation family leadership pushing modernization against incumbent caution
- Major partner or ecosystem shift forcing a repositioning

## Disqualifiers

- Pre-product-market fit — there's no operating model to rewire yet
- Looking for an AI tool implementation, not an operating model change
- Wants a deck, not an outcome — no appetite for execution or measurement
- No executive sponsorship for change
- Below roughly $15M revenue with no sponsor backing
- Consumer-only businesses outside the focus industries
- **Out of lane for now (not a hard disqualifier):** the mandate is *only* operational efficiency or cost reduction, with no growth or revenue angle. See the [2026-09-09 decision](../decisions/decision-log.md).

**Note what is no longer a disqualifier.** Prior positioning excluded "primary ask is growing top-of-funnel or acquiring new customers." Mega flow 4 makes that central to what we do. New revenue and new customers are in scope.
