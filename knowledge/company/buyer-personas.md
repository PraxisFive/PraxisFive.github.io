# Buyer Personas

The [Ideal client profile](ideal-client-profile.md) says *which companies*. This says *which people inside them* — who signs, who champions, who blocks, what each fears, and the words they actually use.

> *Derived from the positioning, the operating model, and the offering architecture. Replace assumed language with quoted language as real discovery calls accumulate — that's what turns this from a derived file into a proprietary one.*

---

## Persona 1 — CEO
*The one accountable for whether the business is built for what's coming.*

**Profile:** Chief executive of a mid-market company in one of the six focus industries, typically sponsor-backed. The business works. The question keeping them up is whether the operating model that got them here survives the AI transition — and whether a competitor rewires first.

**Role in the deal:** **Economic buyer**, and frequently the **champion** too. Operating-model change doesn't happen without them. They can greenlight an S or M in one conversation; L and XL usually need the sponsor aligned.

**Cares most about:** Enterprise value and durability. Being ahead rather than reactive on AI. An answer for the board and the sponsor that holds up. Not spending a year on a study.

**Fears most:** Being the leadership team that watched the transition happen to them. Committing to a large transformation that stalls. Buying a deck. Discovering a competitor moved first and it's already visible in the market.

**Their words:** "we know we need to do something with AI," "the board is asking," "we can't keep operating like this," "where do we even start," "I don't want another consulting report."

**What lands:** "Stuff, not fluff." "We assess all five, then we go deep on the one that matters." "Value shows up in weeks, not at the end." "5x minimum, and we measure it."

**Typically enters at:** M as the default. XS to establish a starting point when the relationship is new. XL when the operating model itself is the scope.

## Persona 2 — The mega flow owner
*The functional executive who owns the area where the value actually is.*

This persona is deliberately variable — **it resolves by dominant mega flow.** The account hypothesis picks the flow, and the flow picks the person.

| Dominant mega flow | Most likely owner |
|---|---|
| 1 — Planning | CFO · Chief Strategy Officer · COO |
| 2 — Innovation & portfolio | Chief Product Officer · Head of Innovation · Business unit GM |
| 3 — Ecosystem | Chief Partner/Alliance Officer · CRO · Chief Strategy Officer |
| 4 — Go-to-market | **CRO · Chief Commercial Officer** first, because they own every route to market. CMO or head of sales only as a way in. |
| 5 — Infrastructure & readiness | COO · CIO/CTO · CHRO · Chief AI or Transformation Officer. **The CIO is also the buyer for AI FinOps.** |

**Why the CRO for GTM** (Lorin, [2026-08-14](../meetings/2026-08-14-gtm-mega-flow-working-session.md)): *"Not chief sales officer, not chief marketing officer … a chief revenue officer owns all the routes to market."* The problems we solve cross marketing, sales, partners and customer success, so the buyer has to own all of them. His read on many CROs is useful for how we pitch: they usually grew up in direct sales, understand alliances and channels less well, and have little patience for marketing. Many are struggling. *"By the time I walk in the door, 70% of the decision has already been made."*

**Their words (CRO / PE):** "repeatable, predictable, durable revenue," "the multiple," "ARR / NRR," "share of wallet," "I've got the playbook" (the inside-sales / SDR model many were raised on, now fatigued), "we need growth."

**The IT buyer** (for FinOps and infrastructure work) is under pressure too. Lorin: *"These IT people need a friend right now … they're getting beat up."* Go in as the ally who makes AI spend visible and controllable, not as a rival to their vendors.

**Role in the deal:** Most often the **champion** — they feel the friction daily and bring us in. Can be the **economic buyer** for an S scoped to their flow. Can also turn **blocker** if an outside read feels like a referendum on their function. The framing has to make PraxisFive their evidence-generator, not their auditor.

**Cares most about:** Fixing the thing they've been unable to fix internally. Defending and expanding the investment case for their area. Evidence their CEO and board respect. Being seen as the one who moved first.

**Fears most:** An external assessment that exposes them rather than vindicates them. Being handed an AI mandate with no resources. Losing budget or headcount in a restructure they didn't shape.

**What lands:** Frame the relationship as: *we hand you the proof — you own the win.* Then speak in their flow's language, not ours.

**Typically enters at:** S scoped to their flow — the fastest path to defensible evidence. M when they can pull the CEO in.

## Persona 3 — The sponsor
*PE operating partner, or family office principal/director.*

**Profile:** Mid-market PE and growth-equity operating partners, and family office directors and principals. Their benches are typically deep in finance, GTM, and product — and thin on AI-era operating model design.

**Role in the deal:** **Economic buyer** for portfolio-level work and post-acquisition value plans. High-leverage **referrer and champion** across the portfolio — once one company runs it, the next is faster, and they can mandate it top-down. Rarely the blocker; more often the accelerant.

**Cares most about:** Protecting and compounding the value of the asset. Repeatability across the portfolio. An independent, IC-grade read. Whether AI is a real value lever here or a distraction.

**Fears most:** A portfolio company falling behind on the transition with no independent read on why. A value plan that doesn't survive IC scrutiny. Spending on AI theater.

**Their words (PE):** "the portco," "value creation plan," "operating partner," "IC," "post-acquisition," "the asset," "repeatable across the portfolio."

**Their words (family office):** "the family business," "the next generation," "continuity," "we've done it this way for thirty years," "we need to modernize without breaking it," "protecting the legacy."

**What lands (PE):** "IC-grade output." "Repeatable across portcos — the second one is faster." "XS to establish a read across the portfolio."

**What lands (family office):** "Modernize without risking the legacy." "Precious with your money." "We work with the next generation and with continuity, not against either."

**Typically enters at:** XS to establish a read across the portfolio. M as the standard value-plan engagement. Continuous AI Realization Service as the portfolio-wide standard once one has run.

## The skeptic / blocker

Rarely a fourth person — usually one of these objections wearing a title:

- **The CFO eyeing the spend.** Wants the math before approving $150K–$350K. Neutralized by the 5x minimum floor, the fast-value tracks landing inside the first weeks, and XS/S as a low-commitment proof point before any larger commitment.
- **"We'll do this ourselves."** Neutralized by the models, tools, and frameworks plus domain expertise — and by the point that an internal team cannot produce an *independent* read on itself.
- **"We already have an AI initiative."** Usually true, and usually tooling rather than operating model. Neutralized by the distinction: they've adopted AI, they haven't rewired for it.
- **The incumbent tier-1 firm.** Neutralized by AI-native delivery economics, speed, and outcomes measured rather than a deck filed.
- **The CIO who already has vendors.** Don't get into a *"pissing match"* over whose tooling is better. It slows the sale. Neutralized by the not-a-VAR boundary: we sit between the client and their VAR and make their existing stack execute ([Tech and partners](tech-and-partners.md)).
- **"AI doesn't pay off."** The default belief in most buyers now. Don't argue with it. Agree, explain why, and show the different approach ([Positioning](praxis-five-positioning.md)).

## The champion pattern

The deal usually gets carried by **Persona 2** — the mega flow owner who feels the friction — or by a personally-exposed **Persona 1**. **Persona 3** can install it top-down. For every account, ask: *who here is most likely to raise their hand?* That's who the outreach should be written to reach.
