# Service Offerings

PraxisFive sells and delivers **one thing in five sizes**, plus **one recurring service in three levels**.

The engagement spine never changes: **Baseline / Assessment / Benchmarks → Roadmap / Blueprints / Action Plans → Execution Tracks.** What changes across sizes is depth, breadth, and duration. See [Engagement architecture](engagement-architecture.md) for the spine itself.

---

## The five sizes

| Size | Price | Positioning |
|------|-------|-------------|
| **XS** | Free | The entry point. A directional read that establishes a starting point and earns the right to a real conversation. |
| **S** | $75K | Focused. One mega flow, or a fast baseline with an actionable near-term roadmap. |
| **M** | ~$150K | The standard engagement. Full baseline across all five mega flows, dominant flow worked in depth, roadmap with execution tracks. |
| **L** | $250K – $350K | Broad and deep. Multiple mega flows worked, execution extending into Critical Priorities and Strategic Plays. |
| **XL** | Custom ($350K+) | Operating-model scope. Transformational Bets on the table. Scoped to the engagement. |

**Sizing logic.** Size is driven by the client's journey stage and the breadth of mega flows in scope — not by company size or budget alone. A Level-1 Emerging client at a large enterprise may correctly be an S. A Level-5 Exponential client at a mid-market company may correctly be an XL.

**Underneath the packaging, pricing is effort-based** — how hard the problem is and how much capacity it consumes. The t-shirt sizes are how we present a time-and-materials reality as a buyable package. XS is the try-and-buy foot in the door; XL is scoped bespoke. The three in the middle are the core of the model. See [Economic model](economic-model.md) for what each size has to cost us.

**S skews sprint-and-project shaped.** The ambition is to escape pure project work into recurring revenue — which is what the Continuous AI Realization Service below exists to do.

## Continuous AI Realization Service

Our recurring advisory and managed service. This is where value realization is sustained after the engagement — and it is the third AI-native pillar made concrete: AI as a lasting, running deliverable, not a document.

| Level | Price | Positioning |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| **Essential** | ~$10K / month | Baseline recurring advisory and managed support. |
| **Plus** | $20K / month | Expanded scope and cadence. |
| **PraxisFive Advantage** | $25K / month | The full offer. Deepest coverage. |

**Why it matters commercially.** It converts project revenue into recurring revenue, keeps us present while value actually compounds, and gives us the longitudinal data that makes PraxisFive a learning company. Realization is measured here, not asserted at the end of a project.

**This is the firm's multiplier.** The project business is the landing motion; this is the model. Sold as **1-year and 3-year subscriptions**, discounted toward the longer term, billed monthly. The commercial goal is explicit: **land clients on Advantage at $25K.** A separate team sits behind this — see [Delivery model](delivery-model.md).

> **To define:** what's included at each level and what specifically differentiates Plus from Advantage — a $5K/month gap is narrow and needs a clear story. Also: minimum term, **mid-subscription exit terms** (undecided), and whether an engagement is a prerequisite. Flagged in [open-questions](../decisions/open-questions.md).

## Packaged services — the way in

**Established 2026-08-05 (session 2).** Outcome-based, fixed-scope entry products that sit ahead of a full engagement. Sized by complexity — **novice → intermediate → advanced**:

| Package | Duration |
|---------|----------|
| **First Step** | 2 days (~16 hours) — workshop and whiteboarding |
| **Jump Start / Fast Path** | ~1 month |
| **Right Start** | 90 days |

**The lesson these are built on.** Cisco's "Expert as a Service" was an open-ended pool of expert hours. It died — *"it was a money pit… there were no outcomes."* The replacement was outcome-based packaging: a fixed deliverable, a fixed scope, a fixed price, with complexity escalating the tier.

### "Free" is really try-and-buy

Marc's position: *"I'm not a fan of doing anything free. It's really optional."* His framing is the car-dealership **puppy dog close** — let them take it for the weekend, because it's hard to give the puppy back.

Lorin's is narrower and compatible: pick **one** problem, deliver something real in a short window, don't charge for it — then the next problem is paid. *"If we don't prove the value, no harm."*

**Always paper it with a $0 statement of work.** It protects the scope and makes the boundary explicit. The XS should be understood as a try-and-buy with a defined outcome, not as open-ended free work.

## How an offering gets built

**Agreed 2026-08-14** ([GTM working session](../meetings/2026-08-14-gtm-mega-flow-working-session.md)). It answers the field feedback that the deck's offerings read as abstract. Every mega flow follows the same recipe:

1. **Explain the space.** Our framework and point of view for the mega flow.
2. **Name about 10 problems** we see in the market (the "flows").
3. **Attach at least one proven use case** to each problem, with a real example.
4. **Package it S / M / L.** The three accelerators, sized.
5. **Add a value sheet or short video** showing how you buy it.

*"It's that simple, isn't it?"* **GTM Activation & Execution is first**. Its draft problem list and use cases are in [AI Integrated Operating Model](ai-operating-model.md). Lorin's working price band for these phase-one packages is **$75K–$125K** (2026-08-13), which brackets the S ($75K) and M (~$150K) sizes above. Treat it as the S/M zone, not a new price point.

**The client pitch this enables:** *"Pay $75K and I deliver $750K. That's a 10x return."* That's illustrative, from Lorin. The contractual floor stays **5x**.

## Not in the lane, for now

- **Operational-efficiency and cost-reduction projects** (claims routing, invoice matching, back-office automation). Lorin, 2026-09-09: *"I don't want to be a cost or productivity dealer."* We lead with growth, revenue and innovation. See [Positioning](praxis-five-positioning.md).
- **Reselling or configuring infrastructure.** We're not a VAR and not in the configuration business. See [Tech and partners](tech-and-partners.md).

Neither is permanent. The point, in Jesse's words, is to *"be clear about what we do and what we don't do right now."* Lorin didn't rule efficiency work out: *"It doesn't mean that I couldn't do operational efficiency."* When a real opportunity shows up, the route in is an Expert Pool specialist (the supply-chain expert was his example), not repositioning the firm.

## What the client actually receives

> *"I can't just deliver a PowerPoint. We have to deliver a digital something. They have to get a digital thumbprint."*

**AI agents are part of the deliverable.** PraxisFive can host them, or help deploy them into the client's own environment — agents sit on-prem where they have to, with the rest hosted. This is the pattern Snowflake, Databricks, and Workday already run, so it is well-trodden rather than novel.

This is the third AI-native pillar made concrete: **what we leave behind.** See [Positioning](praxis-five-positioning.md) and [Tech and partners](tech-and-partners.md).

## The ROI promise

**5x minimum.** That's the floor we stand behind, tied to measured outcomes over a defined horizon — not the ceiling we aim for.

The floor is deliberately defensible rather than aspirational. It has to hold up under CFO scrutiny on a $350K engagement, and it has to be provable through the Continuous AI Realization Service rather than argued in a closing deck.
