# PraxisFive Positioning

> **Brand guidance landed 2026-08-07.** The name, its meaning, the taglines, the canonical narrative, and the visual identity now live in [Brand](brand.md). This file covers what we believe and how we're positioned; [Brand](brand.md) covers how we say it.

## The category

**PraxisFive is an AI-native Outcome Realization firm.**

*Ideas. Execution. Outcomes.*

That category doesn't exist yet. We're defining it. Not an AI consultancy that advises on AI. Not a management consultancy that added an AI practice. A firm whose own operating model, delivery model, and deliverables are all AI-native — organized around one thing: our clients realizing value.

## What we believe

**AI is the most significant technological advancement of our time.** It changes how we live, how we work, and how we play. Capturing that opportunity isn't a matter of adding tools to an existing business. It requires rethinking and rewiring the business itself.

**Professional services is among the most disrupted industries there is — and it's being disrupted from the inside out.** We say that as participants, not spectators. There are parts of the legacy management consulting model that are genuinely excellent, and we take full advantage of them. Everything else we rethink from first principles, because the old delivery economics can no longer justify themselves.

**Human intelligence plus artificial intelligence is where the magic happens.** The work is finding the balance — knowing where HI and AI each do their best work. AI carries scale, speed, and pattern surface area. Humans carry judgment, act as the glue, see the patterns *across*, and connect the dots — including the dots nobody else noticed were there.

**What we bring that can't be prompted:** our models, tools, and frameworks, combined with access to data and real domain experience and expertise. That combination is the asset. The models alone aren't.

## What makes us AI-native — the three pillars

AI-native isn't a claim about our tooling. It's true in three distinct places, and we work on all three every day.

**1. How we operate.** Internally — as a team, as a culture, as an organization — AI is in every aspect of what we do.

**2. How we engage and deliver.** Every part of how we engage clients and how we deliver the work is built on AI.

**3. What we leave behind.** AI is part of what our clients keep. In the artifacts we deliver — agents among them — and in the continuous recurring services we run for them, which are AI at the core.

A firm can claim any one of these. Claiming all three is the position.

## The learning company

This is what we love most about AI: it lets us build a genuinely learning company. We get better as the models get better, and better again as we learn more. That compounding is not a side effect — it's an essential part of how we work with clients and how we evolve as a business.

And it isn't only about new technology arriving. It's our own company learning continuously, from every engagement, and carrying that forward. We treat that as a durable strategic advantage.

## The ethos — four things true of every engagement

Every PraxisFive engagement is:

1. **Contextually relevant** — built for this client, this market, this moment. Never a template with the logo swapped.
2. **Outcome-based** — measured by what the client realizes, not by what we deliver.
3. **Fast value** — value shows up early, not at the end of a long study.
4. **Minimum 5x ROI** — the floor we stand behind, not the ceiling we aim for.

**We want to be known as the firm that has the stuff, not the fluff.**

## The North Star

**Client value realization.** Our client is always our North Star. Every mega flow, every offering, every recurring service resolves to that question: did the client actually realize the value?

## Velocity, not just speed

Acceleration is speed. **Velocity is speed with direction.** That distinction runs through everything we do — eliminating friction and getting clients to the fastest time to value, pointed the right way. Speed in the wrong direction is just expensive motion.

## Our point of view: why AI isn't paying off, and why we can make it

> Built by the team on [2026-09-09](../meetings/2026-09-09-website-and-engagement-framework.md), with inputs from [2026-08-11](../meetings/2026-08-11-design-and-brand-working-session.md) and [2026-08-14](../meetings/2026-08-14-gtm-mega-flow-working-session.md). Jesse's prompt: *"AI consultancies and agencies are starting to get a bad rap … we need to hit that head on."* This is the core of the website and outreach story.

**The headline: it isn't working.** Money is going out, adoption isn't following, and the returns aren't showing up. Buyers already believe this.

**Why:**
- **It's bolted on.** Tools get layered onto processes nobody examined, built around how the job is *supposed* to be done rather than how it's actually done.
- **The rollout had no structure.** *"Here's Copilot, go play with it."* Licences everywhere, nothing embedded, nothing measured.
- **The costs are hidden.** Token, data-access and harness costs nobody sees or audits. *"It's all being eyeballed."*
- **The process was never documented.** No process, no agent. Model choice is a small fraction of whether a project survives. Most of it is whether the workflow was understood first.
- **Adoption is treated as the user's job.** People are asked to learn a new way to work, instead of the new way running underneath the work they already do.

**What we do instead:**
- **Redesign the work, not just the tools.** This is business process reengineering, renewed for AI. *"A 15-step process now is a four-step process."*
- **Document the real process first.** Reconstruct it from completed work, including the exceptions and the judgment calls someone carries in their head.
- **Experts work below the surface.** Employees keep doing their jobs and handle the three exceptions instead of a hundred repetitions. No citizen-developer free-for-all, and no licence spray.
- **Make the costs visible and governed.** Role-based access, token allowances, an AI gateway (see [Tech and partners](tech-and-partners.md)).
- **Show value in weeks, then keep going.** Start with the lowest-lift, highest-value box, not the high-risk moonshot where most programs stall.
- **Executives get their hands dirty.** In the new model, leaders are close to the work again.

**The line:** *"We're the company that makes it work."* (Marc, 2026-09-09). A candidate, not adopted. See [Voice and style](voice-and-style.md).

> ⚠️ **Proof points need sources before external use.** In circulation: the MIT study on AI pilots failing; HBR on negative ROI; "6%" / "14% of CEOs" reporting a return (the team quoted both); and a practitioner article's **86%** of agent pilots never reaching production, **55%** of executives calling their AI use chaotic, **only 16%** with streamlined cross-functional workflows, and a **3:1** documentation-to-build ratio. Lorin: *"I don't know where he's getting those numbers."* See [assumptions](../decisions/assumptions.md).

### What buyers are tired of

*"The consultant who takes the watch off your arm and then tells you what time it is."* Clients want an **action-based firm that arrives with a point of view** and puts skin in the game to prove or disprove the value quickly. Not the endless discovery-meeting sales cycle. And not the AI shops that are *"drive-bys … in and out"*. We want engagements to go so well that clients *"can't imagine not working with us on the next thing."*

## What's different about the scope

We look at the **full lifecycle of a business** — new revenue *and* existing revenue, new customers *and* existing customers.

Optimizing existing customers and existing revenue quality remains a core strength and a real part of what we do. It's no longer the boundary of what we do.

### Our lane, for now: the revenue side

**Lorin, [2026-09-09](../decisions/decision-log.md):** *"I don't want to be a cost or productivity dealer … We want to be on the revenue generation side of the problem … growth and innovation."* Operational-efficiency projects (claims routing, invoice matching) are **out of lane for now**, even when they're good projects. We also don't resell or configure infrastructure ([Tech and partners](tech-and-partners.md)).

This narrows *where we lead*, not what the model covers. All five mega flows still get assessed. The lane decides which findings we take on ourselves. Jesse's rule: be explicit about what we do and don't do right now. One exception is deliberate: **AI FinOps** is cost work, but it sits with the IT buyer as a strategic service, not as back-office efficiency.
