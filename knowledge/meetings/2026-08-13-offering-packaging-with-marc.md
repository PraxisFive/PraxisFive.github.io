## What Lorin needs: services math, kept simple

- *"We're in the services business, not a product company."* An offering breaks down into **activities → deliverables → hours × expertise.**
- **Two core deliverables:** (1) a **baseline assessment**, the current-state analysis, and (2) a **blueprint** of how they get from today to their stated destination. The blueprint includes a **priority map**: what could deliver results in *hours, days, weeks or months*.
- **Phase one = baseline → plan → execute**, packaged as offerings **between $75K and $125K**. Build a generic version first, then customize it for each of the five mega flows.
- **Assessment tools:** interview guides plus an "old school e-survey kind of thing." *"Between you and me, I can do that stuff without anything … get four people in a room."*

### The feature-release story, in Lorin's retelling

This is Lorin's version of Jesse's use case, under the Innovation & Product Development flow. Product managers draw an "above the line / below the line" feature roadmap. Signals from the field can make a below-the-line feature urgent. *"If seven sales teams said the same thing, that's a pattern."*

Jesse heard that **global currency** was blocking deals. He reprioritized, got it built **in a week**, and the business **closed two deals at $500K each within four weeks**. Pricing, website and collateral were all updated in the same two weeks.

*"That's a tactical win, not a transformation … Pay me $75,000 and I deliver $750,000, that's a 10x return. There's not a team that's not going to say yes to that."*

**But strategic IT services are in.** Lorin: *"This token thing is a service we want … having a framework, helping them. To me, that is such a value add."* IT leaders "need a friend right now," because *"they're getting beat up."*
