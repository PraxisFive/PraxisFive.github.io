## Substantive outputs

### 1. The product is a chain, not a document
**Value Assessment → Roadmap → Execution Tracks**, integrated so each stage feeds the next automatically. Lorin has built this shape before and wants it rebuilt properly: *"I want this to all be part of one."* This is the engagement spine rendered as tooling rather than as deliverables.

### 2. Execution track horizons — compressed, and now confirmed
Stated explicitly and corroborated by the board: **hours/days → days/weeks → weeks → month → month-plus.** Materially shorter than the working draft previously held in the vault (days → weeks → quarters → multi-quarter → multi-year). Consistent with the rest of the session's compression thesis. *Carried into [Engagement architecture](../company/engagement-architecture.md); the oddity of "month-plus" for a Transformational Bet is flagged in open-questions.*

### 3. Pods are the delivery unit — measured in FTE, not headcount
Three tiers: **Client Pod** (core, employee-weighted) · **Expert Pod** (certified, mixed 1099/employee) · **Talent Network** (all 1099, as-needed, offshore-weighted). Lorin: *"I tend to think of this as FTE, not necessarily people... What's the delivery unit?"* Marc defined it: one FTE = one full-time level of effort; a mandate is a fixed hour block.

The client pod collapsed to **three roles** by the end of the session — Engagement Manager (= project manager, explicitly the same person), Technical Analyst, Strategist/Domain Expert — with admin support and possible EM/strategist merge. Lorin's reaction: *"I'm loving how simple this is. Small. I like it."*

### 4. The EM/PM hire spec is now concrete
Poached from agencies at 40–60K · landing ~**80K** with an AI-native premium · **EQ and problem-solving over raw intellect** (Marc's filter) · no CS degree required but genuinely technical · **probabilistic rather than deterministic thinking** (Marc's test: *"I give you a theoretical scenario, you tell me what the outcome looks like"*) · AI-native by habit, not by certification.

### 5. Economics
30% EBITDA at the firm. Project cost target **~50% of price** ($75K job should cost ≤$35K). **$350/hr blended average**, down from a $500 aspiration, with an internal three-tier rate card. Book of business **$1.5–2.5M per pod**; 2–3 pods for a $5M firm. The "3X model" invoked against MBB's 10–14X — *definition ambiguous in the source, flagged.*

### 6. IP retention is a contracting stance, not an afterthought
The strongest single thread in the session. Clients get derivative rights and can make the models their own; **Praxis Five retains the right to reuse its own frameworks — including with a client's direct competitors** — absent an explicit named restriction. Lorin's test case: if we build something for one client, we should be free to do the same work for its direct competitor, unless that client has named the competitor in the contract.

Reuse is also the **margin mechanism**: *"For us to make money, we got to be able to reuse stuff."*

### 7. Assessment methodology carries over from alliances
Four levels of assessment object — **Partner · Portfolio · Program · People** — scaling from one entity to thousands, quant (e-surveys) plus qual (interview guides). Portable to any object: partners, clients, reps, sales teams.

### 8. Selling compresses
Hypothesis-and-POV led. Homework → ICP + target account → meeting → proposal *or* one more discovery meeting. Warm intro beats cold. Scarcity posture (Jesse): *"We're selective, but we want to work with you."* Lorin: *"I don't want to spend six months selling."*

### 9. The flywheel
Selling ↔ CX/Client Delivery ↔ Continuation Services, tightly integrated — described as a helix. Delivery insight feeds selling; the continuous service surfaces new opportunities that become new sprints. Marc owns the CX/observability end; Lorin sees the recurring service as **the firm's valuation multiplier**.

### 10. Build non-bespoke
Use cases built in client work are deliberately **built non-bespoke** so they become reusable assets.

### 11. The planning thesis (why mega flow 1 exists)
Plans today are static — they sit on the shelf, disconnected from headcount, investment, resources, and targets, with measurement that arrives too late and data that isn't trustworthy. The counter-position: plans should be **live, integrated, and measured against actuals in real time.**
