## "No process, no agent": the article Jesse brought

A practitioner article Jesse sent round. Lorin: *"It's on brand … 100%."* The claims below are **quoted from the article and not independently sourced.** Lorin: *"I don't know where he's getting those numbers."*

- **Model selection accounts for about 5%** of whether an AI project survives. The other 95% is process.
- **A 3:1 ratio:** documenting the workflow takes about three times longer than building the agent.
- **86%** of AI agent pilots never reach production scale.
- **55%** of executives describe their company's AI use as a "chaotic free-for-all."
- **Only 16%** of enterprises have streamlined their cross-functional workflows. The other 84% deploy agents against fragmented or undocumented processes. Lorin: *"That's us. Remember, cross-functional workflow."*
- **AI sidekicks vs background agents: 1:10** in enterprise value. Background agents run on their own and surface only exceptions and judgment calls. Efficiency on the workflows they take on is 60–90%.
- **"Ask Janet"**: if the step lives in one person's head and can't be described, it can't be automated.

**What the team took from it:**
- Jesse: we need **a tool and a process for documenting a client's workflow**. We reconstruct the real process from completed work, not from what people say they do. *"How it should be done and how it's actually done are two different things."* He wants Marc to help build a lightweight internal tool.
- Lorin: this is his 20 years of process modelling, and his data-warehousing practice of starting from "what are your most complex questions?" **The floor value of any engagement:** *"The worst you get at the end of it is we documented it for you."*
- Lorin also wants **Marc's "seven human things"** and **his own "seven AI risks"** used alongside it. Neither is in the vault yet.

## The three capabilities to build

Lorin: *"At the end of the day there's really three things that we gotta have an ability to do."*

1. **Front-end analysis** (benchmark, assessment, diagnostic, "I don't care what we call it"). It comes in different flavours but is the same underneath. Interview guides by function are the core (for example, the guide for a CMO interview). **Brian is strong at interview guides and could contribute here.**
2. **A documented blueprint output.** Gantt-style plans, month by month: *"you're going from here to here, and this month these are the five things you need to do, and these are the actions, and here's the owners."* Automate as much of it as possible.
3. **Verbatims and unstructured data.** *"Executives love verbatims."* Transcripts are data. They must feed the plan, and governance has to be able to drill back to qualitative evidence, not just numbers.

**Market signal:** "headless CRM," meaning AI in front of the CRM with the CRM as system of record. Meeting notes becoming a record brings new accountability to alliances and sales. Gong transcripts were the early version of this.
